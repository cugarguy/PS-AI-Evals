## Method + Ground Truth (two ✅/⚠️ cells)

### Hallucination Rate
- **Method:** An automated claim-to-source check: compare the summary’s material factual claims, figures, and named entities against the source report. This could combine deterministic checks for numbers/entities with an LLM-as-Judge or entailment-style evaluator for whether the claims are supported.
- **Ground truth:** A curated set of representative source research reports, paired with human-reviewed expected facts or reference summaries. The source report itself is the authority; a pass means the output does not introduce material unsupported claims.

### Latency
- **Method:** Automated end-to-end latency monitoring. Instrument the summary-generation request and measure the time from submission of a report to a completed summary; monitor a percentile such as p95, not merely the average.
- **Ground truth:** The agreed product SLA/SLO for summary delivery, ideally defined by report-size band. For example: “Summaries of typical client reports must return within the approved experience target.” Use the actual target if the scenario gives you one—do not invent a number.

## Strategic acceptance

**Accepted gap:** Drift Monitoring (Robustness)

We are temporarily accepting the absence of dedicated drift monitoring during the initial launch period. The product begins with a relatively bounded report domain and unchanged model and retrieval configuration, so we are prioritizing the more immediate risk of biased or incomplete representation in client-facing summaries. This is a time-bounded decision, not a claim that drift is low-risk indefinitely. We will add dedicated drift monitoring by [date], or sooner if the report mix expands, source formats materially change, the model or retrieval pipeline changes, or evidence of declining source-grounded quality emerges.

## Critical mitigation

**Critical gap:** Bias (Fairness)

**Why critical:** We cannot accept a dedicated Bias/Fairness gap because report summaries may systematically emphasize or omit perspectives across geographies, industries, or company types. Because the summaries are delivered to clients and may inform decisions, the harm can be subtle, difficult to detect, and damaging to trust.

**Mitigation plan:** Implement a comparative fairness evaluator using a stratified, SME-reviewed report dataset across key regions, industries, company sizes, and report types. Run it before material releases and conduct weekly sampled-production review at launch, moving to monthly expert audits once stable. Refresh the dataset whenever the source mix changes or a new representation risk is found.
