Strategy · M1

What "good" means

Target user

Who + use case

VP-level strategists and product leaders at Fortune 500 companies; extract verified competitor-pricing and review insights for roadmap decisions.
Definition of good

The user promise

Verified, citation-backed answers in about five seconds, with up to ten seconds for additional validation. These are the M1 proposed experience targets, not measured latency results.
Trust metrics

Top 3

Hallucination Rate · Robustness · Latency (P95/P99)
Trade-offs

What we chose

1. Hallucination Rate over Robustness: avoid unsupported claims even when not every phrasing can be handled.
2. Hallucination Rate over Latency: verification takes priority for high-stakes enterprise decisions.

Decision · M5 + M6

Ship or hold

HOLD · recorded gates do not support enterprise release
The answer

Recommendation

I recommend we HOLD Ascend IQ because the recorded P0 grounding gate is blocked and the evaluator is not calibrated well enough to support an enterprise launch.
Three arguments

Why

1. P0 grounding remains below the release bar. PR #218 records faithfulness at 87 against a floor of 95, down 9 points from main against a maximum regression of 5. The M3 pricing case still quotes $49 where the verified reference says $59 and adds an unverified 10-seat minimum. Detecting this failure does not demonstrate that it has been fixed.

2. The quality evidence does not yet support a reliable go/no-go decision. Judge calibration records Cohen’s κ of -0.29 against the required 0.60, with 6 disagreements across 12 labels. The trajectory tool records only 1/6 dimensions passed and a HOLD verdict after the agent omitted ingestion verification. These are completed lab results, with failed readiness checks.

3. Portfolio spending does not close the critical coverage gap. M5 allocates $180,000 to three Level 3 selections under the $200,000 cap. The external report-summary coverage row still has no dedicated Bias/Fairness evaluator and only partial hallucination coverage. Dedicated drift monitoring is an accepted temporary gap; the date remains unspecified. Weekly bias audit is a fallback plan, not proof of measured fairness.

Reflection

What I learned

Realization

Subjective to objective

Defining “good enough” made the distinction between a completed evaluation and a passing release visible. A judge can catch the pricing P0 in one example and still fail calibration across 12 labels. A portfolio can stay within budget while retaining a critical fairness gap. The existing strategy prioritizes verified claims over speed; the final decision must apply that priority even when it delays release.
Next sprint

What I attack first

Repair pricing/grounding, rerun calibration and the trajectory, and verify the critical fairness mitigation before reconsidering release.
